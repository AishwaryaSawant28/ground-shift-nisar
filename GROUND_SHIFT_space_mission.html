The minimum radar science you need to read this case honestly. Definitions follow the NASA source and standard InSAR explanations.

Glossary

8 entries
SAR — Synthetic Aperture Radar
Instrument

An imaging radar that sends its own microwave pulses and records the returning signal. Because it provides its own illumination, it can observe day or night and through cloud. A “synthetic aperture” is built by combining many pulses as the satellite moves.

NISAR — NASA–ISRO SAR
Mission

A joint NASA–ISRO Earth-observation radar mission. Its L-band radar (about 24 cm wavelength) is designed to measure subtle changes of the land surface, including ground deformation, over large areas. The case uses one published NISAR-derived result, not a live data feed.

InSAR — Interferometric SAR
Method

A technique that compares the phase of radar returns collected at different times from nearly the same viewing geometry. Small changes in the distance between the satellite and the ground shift the phase. Differences between acquisitions produce an interferogram, from which displacement along the radar line of sight can be estimated.

Interferogram
Product

The interference pattern formed by combining two radar acquisitions. Its repeating fringe pattern encodes phase differences. A full phase cycle corresponds to a change in range of half the radar wavelength — for L-band, roughly 12 cm of path difference. Turning fringes into metres requires careful processing and assumptions.

Subsidence
Observation

The slow downward settling of the ground surface. In this case the published map reports areas of Mexico City sinking by more than 2 centimetres per month during the observation period. Subsidence is a measurement of motion, not by itself an explanation of cause.

Displacement
Measurement

Change in position. Radar interferometry measures the component of that change along the satellite's line of sight — part vertical, part horizontal, depending on geometry. A single viewing geometry does not give the full three-dimensional motion.

Residual noise
Uncertainty

Signal artefacts left after processing that are not reliable ground-motion measurements. NASA describes yellow and red patches in this preliminary product as likely residual noise. Noise does not mean the whole map is wrong; it means those specific patches should not be over-interpreted.

Groundwater extraction
Cause

Pumping water from aquifers. Mexico City draws much of its water from the aquifer beneath the former lakebed. Removing water lowers pore pressure in the clay-rich sediments, allowing them to compact. This compaction is a leading explanation for the city's long-term subsidence.

What this map cannot tell you. A radar displacement product does not certify a building as unsafe, does not identify individual damaged structures, and does not prove a single cause. It shows surface motion over a period, with uncertainty and areas of likely noise. Scientific conclusions here combine the observed deformation with published geology and water-use context, and are labelled as inference.
